1. The Symbol as a Weapon: How a Manipulator Captures Your Attention

In modern relations between people, especially in the informational and psychological space, one of the most subtle and insidious forms of influence frequently manifests — manipulation through symbols. The manipulator builds an entire system in which the object of manipulation, the person being manipulated, becomes merely a puppet in a complex game where everything — from words to signs and gestures — is subordinated to a single design: to elicit the desired reaction.

The manipulator does not act chaotically. He does not simply toss random symbols into the information field. He conducts an analysis, seeking out precisely those symbols that evoke sensitive emotional and mental responses in a person. Then these symbols — whether phrases, images, or subtle hints — are deliberately injected into the perceptual field of the person being manipulated. The goal is to initiate a reaction that allows the manipulator to strengthen control, alter the perception of reality, and steer thoughts and feelings in a direction advantageous to himself.

If the person being manipulated succumbs to this influence and reacts directly — emotionally or even rationally — he enters the manipulator's game. He becomes a prisoner of the system, which lives and develops precisely at the expense of such reactions. Manipulation turns into a vicious circle, where each new symbol is injected in expectation of the next expected reaction, and each reaction confirms the effectiveness of the strategy.

1.1. Awareness as the Breaking Point of the System

However, this system has a weak spot, its Achilles' heel — awareness. Manipulation is possible only under conditions of unawareness, in the blind zone of the person being manipulated. As soon as a person moves to the meta-level, stops reacting to symbols as self-contained meanings, and begins to see and describe the very process of manipulative injection, the structure of the system collapses.

Awareness is the first step toward freedom. It is necessary not merely to refuse direct reaction, but to critically expose the mechanism by which the manipulator selects symbols, constructs narratives, and implants them into consciousness. It is important to identify the strategy, not to argue with its particular manifestations. This means — not to get carried away by polemics with every injected word or image, but to move on to a phenomenological analysis of the very technology of influence.

1.2. The Antisymbolic Strategy

Thus, the effective response for a person being manipulated is the antisymbolic strategy. This is a method in which it is not the content of the symbol that becomes the object of struggle, but the very system of its selection and injection that becomes the object of critical examination. The manipulator acts in the shadows, where his tricks remain unnoticed, but as soon as they are exposed and named, their magic disappears, and they turn into a mere set of empty signs devoid of power.

One could say that the person exposing the manipulation becomes a kind of philosopher of practice, a researcher of the psychotechnics of control. He transfers the confrontation from the emotional and subjective level to the analytical one, where there is no place for spontaneous reactions and passions. This is a difficult but necessary path toward restoring the autonomy of consciousness.

1.3. Manipulation as a Scientific Task

In this perspective, manipulation ceases to be a personal tragedy and turns into a scientific task — the task of decoding and neutralizing the algorithms of influence. Conscious criticism of the system of manipulation not only liberates a person but also breaks the chain through which the manipulator transmits his influence further.

Thereby the antisymbolic strategy is a philosophical and practical weapon in the war for one's own freedom and integrity. It teaches one not to fear hidden influences, but to meet them with clarity of understanding and intellectual fortitude.

Only in this way can one escape the trap where every symbol turns into shackles, and every reaction — into an involuntary confirmation of another's power.

2. The Alienation of the Symbol from the Myth in Order to Introduce the Symbol into a Manipulative Pseudo-Myth

The alienation of the symbol from the myth in order to introduce the symbol into a manipulative pseudo-myth and to influence its bearer through it — to achieve a predictable reaction.

Yes, I understand, it sounds convoluted. Let us break it down point by point.

2.1. Choosing the Symbol

Let us imagine that there is a certain myth, and its expression — say — is the image of the main hero of this myth. Let us call it "the symbol."

What does the manipulator do in this manipulative scheme of logic?

First of all — he calculates the symbol to which the victim is sensitive. Calculated. Let it be Christ, for example (we take the symbol purely as an example; children of Allah, do not be offended — and may His name be glorified forever and ever!).

↑ Back to contents

2.2. The Alienation of the Symbol from the Myth

Next, the manipulator alienates the symbol from the myth that stands behind it.

Important: this is not so much about the church myth, not about the history of Christianity as such, as about the individual spiritual experience of the victim connected with the veneration of this symbol. That is, in this context the myth is the genuine personal experience of faith, spiritual experiences, prayers, following ethics, tragedies and victories through which the person has passed with Christ in his heart.

But the manipulator takes this symbol and, what is fundamental, not with a sincere desire to understand what it means for the person, but — with the desire to make an instrument out of it. The symbol is alienated from the myth. The little Semitic figure painted on a board is torn away from the tears of repentance and the joy of the feat of self-sacrifice.

For the manipulator this is — simply a symbol from a database: "Object No. 132, symbol No. 21 'Christ'." This is a string, by pulling which one can achieve an emotional, spontaneous reaction.

↑ Back to contents

2.3. Introducing the Pseudo-Myth

If one simply pulls the string arbitrarily — there will be no use. The reaction may be strong, but it will be unpredictable and uncontrollable. And the manipulator's goal is control. Or, at a minimum — testing and refining the technology of control.

Therefore the manipulator constructs a pseudo-myth around the symbol. Partly — on the basis of the real myth (what he knows about Christianity, about the spiritual history of the victim). But along with this — he introduces manipulative, parasitic elements. For example:

"What, Christ? — Oh yes, we honor Christ. And he said: the prodigal son must repent and return to the father. So, I am your father. Return to me."

Although in essence this is not about the repentance of the prodigal son, but about a victim who has realized that she is being used and has begun to send the manipulator to three cheerful letters (thanks to Saints Cyril and Methodius for inventing these letters).

That is: the symbol — seems to be the same, but it is cut off from its genuine context. Clothed in a dead, synthetic pseudo-myth, saturated with hidden influence.

↑ Back to contents

2.4. Introducing It into the Victim's Mind

The main thing — is to introduce this pseudo-myth into the victim's mind so that she perceives it as her own.

The "friend/foe" mechanism is exploited through the symbol — the key point of her meaning system.

The victim must react: "Oh, this is my symbol — which means the myth standing behind it is mine too."

That's it. The client is ready.

2.5. The Expected Reaction

The victim performs the behavioral act according to the implanted scheme.
The manipulator — observes, records, corrects.

This basic scheme can be modified — for example, to elicit a negative reaction:

  • Introducing a symbol dear to the heart into a discrediting myth — in order to cause pain.
  • Introducing a hated symbol into a glorifying pseudo-myth — in order to evoke disgust and disappointment.

But the essence remains the same: the victim takes the pseudo-myth for the genuine one, and it begins to displace, substitute, or deform her true inner myth.

2.6. The Achilles' Heel of the Scheme

It works only against those who have no genuine experience, no path traversed, no deeply lived personal meanings. A person who has not undergone initiation through suffering, pain, and feat cannot distinguish a counterfeit from the genuine, because he has no standard of comparison.

And if the experience is there?

If it is — sooner or later the manipulation fails. The person will see: before him is not one who tries to understand, but one who plays, presses buttons, pulls strings, processes like an algorithm.

Genuine experience will compare the living with the dead and see the falsity. Will see that before him is not fire but a light bulb. Not love but an emulation. Not light but a shop window.

The manipulator breaks against a heart that has accomplished a feat. And more than once.

The feat is genuine. Sincere. Accomplished.
Such a heart — will be keen-sighted.

The deeper your real experience, the more honestly you have reflected on it (with reason, and with pain, and without escaping from yourself) — the more resilient you are. More resilient to those who build a scheme of your soul in order to poke needles into it.

Live, reflecting on every step.

3. The Alienation of the Symbol from the Myth: The Jobs Case in the False Myth of Spontaneous Insight

Earlier I described the technology of manipulation itself; now let us examine it on a concrete example:

3.1. Introduction

Modern manipulators of consciousness increasingly resort to the strategy of alienating cultural symbols from their historical and biographical contexts. This technique allows them to implant a false myth into the victim's mind, masking ideological goals under supposedly generally accepted images. One of the most striking examples of such an operation is the transformation of the image of Steve Jobs — from a pragmatic technocrat and authoritarian manager — into the figure of a romanticized madman-prophet, supposedly opening access to "insights," "intuition," and "the transcendent."

↑ Back to contents

3.2. The Original Symbol: Steve Jobs as a Technocratic Visionary

Steve Jobs was obsessed with control, minimalism, technological stability, and business results. He did not encourage freedom of thought as a value in itself — he encouraged precision, perfectionism, total control over all details, including the appearance of a bolt in an invisible compartment of the case.

He opposed chaos, amateur self-activity, and even open systems.

Jobs was not a proponent of open code, collective creativity, or transpersonal mysticism.

His successes are the result of a rigid hierarchy, the suppression of dissent, and a brilliant but despotic coordination of engineers and designers.

This is the original myth that has developed around his biography.

↑ Back to contents

3.3. Manipulative Alienation: Separating the Symbol from Biographical Truth

At the first stage of manipulation, the image of Jobs is torn away from the facts. His love of Zen, meditation, and nonstandard thinking is mentioned — without clarifying that he used these as instruments of psychophysical concentration, not as a cult of madness or mysticism.

A quote from Apple's Think Different advertising campaign is torn out of its marketing context.

The words: "only madmen change the world" — are presented as Jobs's personal creed, rather than as an advertising trick invented by the agency Chiat/Day.

Thus the symbol begins to lose its contour, loses its connection with a concrete historical personality.

↑ Back to contents

3.4. Introduction into the False Myth: Rewriting Meaning Under a New Ideology

The next stage — is the embedding of the alienated symbol into a new ideological matrix. In our case — into the myth of a spiritually gifted madman who, through intuitive insights, spontaneous revelations, shamanic practices, and meditations, attains truth.

Jobs is presented as a man who changed the world not thanks to business logic, but in spite of it, thanks to "turning off the mind."

His systematic nature, authoritarianism, and engineering-commercial approach are deliberately hushed up.

Madness begins to be interpreted as a spiritual value, rather than as a risk or an illness.

The symbol becomes the bearer of an idea initially foreign to it.

↑ Back to contents

3.5. Introducing the False Myth into the Audience's (Victim's) Mind

The last stage — is the presentation of this symbol to the victim as a "role model." This is done by appealing to her vulnerabilities:

  • Love for the IT sphere and technology — to use iconic figures from this field in order to evoke trust or interest.
  • Intellectual pride — to appeal to the desire to seem advanced, smart, and on the wave of modern trends.
  • Skepticism toward mass culture and the mainstream — to evoke rejection of "commercial" or "simplified" solutions.

As a result, through the introduction of the pseudo-myth into the victim's mind via its acceptance on the basis of recognizing the symbol (the figure of Jobs) as "hers," an ideal psychotype of a suggestible adept is created — one who wants to be a genius not through labor and discipline, but through a miracle, revelation, inner flow.

The new formula: "Be like Jobs — meditate, turn off your mind, trust madness."

The manipulator gains the right to suggest: what to do, how to feel, where to direct attention.

3.6. Conclusion: The Danger of Rewriting Symbols

The Jobs case is not an exception. It is a typical scheme of creating a false myth, where:

  • A recognizable symbol is taken (technocrat, engineer, scientist);
  • Biographical reality is separated from it;
  • It is inserted into a new scheme of "the mad seer";
  • The symbol is implanted into people's heads in order to impose on them the desired form of thinking — in this case, the rejection of rationality in favor of pseudo-spiritual obedience to "intuition" (in reality — the voice of a guru or a manipulator).

The result: this is not veneration of Jobs's personality, but the exploitation of his brand to introduce anti-rational ideology into the minds of the naive or the seeking.
