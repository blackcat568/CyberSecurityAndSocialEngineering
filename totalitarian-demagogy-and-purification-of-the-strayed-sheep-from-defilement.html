Totalitarian Demagogy in the Guise of Spiritual Purification of the Strayed Sheep from Defilement

There is a particular kind of evil that smiles. It does not shout: "Kill and obey!" — it whispers: "Love, repent, heal." This is not a form of aggression, but a form of humble violence. Not a club to the head, but a gentle deception, aiming to purge from you any thought that does not align with the dogma. And when this demagogy dons the mantle of spirituality — we are dealing with a new type of totalitarian construct: a pseudo-spiritual dictatorship, or — in 21st-century terms — a soft cult of control.

In this cult, heads are not cut off — the doubters are "healed." But healing does not consist in relieving pain, but in burning out critical thinking.

Exposure = a Symptom of Illness

Suppose you pointed out a lie. With facts, with examples, with logic. You presented documents, revealed manipulation schemes, showed how the "spiritual leader" covers greed with words about love. And this is what you hear in response:

"You are simply wounded. It's all from pain. You have trauma. You have an internal dependency. You do not realize that you are fighting yourself, not us."

The argument is not refuted — it is annulled, devalued. Your criticism is not analyzed because your entire personality is questioned. This is the translation of a dispute into the realm of psychiatry. A dispute is impossible with someone who is "sick." To challenge obscurantism is to be a "duped sheep, led by trauma."

This is the trick: replacing logical analysis with moral discreditation. You are wrong because you are corrupt. And being corrupt — means you do not need to listen, you need to pray, meditate, and repent before the "kind elder."

"Struggle" as a Symptom of Dependency

The next step — implanting the belief that the very act of actively fighting falsehood is already a disease. A truly healthy person, according to this system, does not struggle. They are calm, balanced, smiling, and accept the world. That is, they accept lies, corruption, manipulations — and smile.

If you call things by their names — you are "aggressive." And if you are aggressive — then you have a problem with the "second center," or with childhood, or with your father, or with your liver. There is a problem. Not with them. But with you. Because you do not want to pray.

This is the rhetoric of any totalitarian regime: the enemy of the system is not an enemy, but a sick person. They must not be listened to, but treated. Their protest is not a protest, it is a symptom. Their correctness is just a feature of the diagnosis.

The Sect in "Salvation" Mode

When you accuse them of deceit — they call you to "healing." When you expose their lies — they call you to "love." But in reality — this is a refusal of equal dialogue, a refusal of arguments and ethics.

You are the "strayed sheep." Your friends are "heretics." And the criticism itself is not exposure, but a descent into dark passions. Admit it, repent, sever ties with friends, return to the community. Renounce reason, gain peace.

But in reality — peace will come only when you surrender. When you stop thinking.

Even if the sect has caused a member moral harm, as a result of which they no longer wish to interact with the organization — since they see no point in exposing themselves to further suffering — the sect's leadership will respond: "You are not steadfast enough in purifying your spirit, in overcoming pride, and in humility. A truly strong-spirited person will continue the path of obedience and purification."

In other words, the person is required to endure humiliation and abuse, and this is presented as spiritual maturity. The desire to abandon practices incompatible with modern ethics, current law, and elementary common sense is interpreted not as a sign that something is wrong with the organization, but as a personal weakness of the member themselves.

In reality, something else lies behind this: the person continues to be cynically exploited, and any attempt to break free from control is explained away as their "insufficient moral fortitude" and "disobedience." Thus, responsibility for the leadership's mistakes and abuses is shifted onto the victim — a classic inversion of responsibility.

Retention in a Sect Through Unethical and Unlawful Methods

To keep a person inside a sect, the following mechanism is often used — the classic cultic anchoring to a "choice once made." For example, authoritarianism and violence — a ban on activities that interest the person and the imposition of what benefits the sect's leadership — are presented as a "practice of humility," for which the victim themselves should be grateful.

An example of what the teacher allegedly said to the head of one of the destructive sects I have studied:

"You chose this yourself, why did you go there? I chose for myself the path of obedience, of monastic life, and that was it. Once, it was my conscious choice. No one broke me, no one forced me... Sometimes my dado would humble me. He comes and says: 'Do you like this work?' I say: 'Yes, I like it.' He says: 'From this day on, I give you the opportunity to do this work for only 15 minutes a day.' I think: 'What a bastard, how can anyone do that?' ...But I am so grateful to him, because afterward I had a happy and harmonious life, because this humility came."

That is, the head of the sect claims that he was "humbled" with his own consent, and he is grateful to his teacher — humility made him better, and this is supposedly an example of how one should behave.

This is the mechanism of cultic retention, spoken aloud by the manipulator himself.

  • Anchoring to a one-time choice. "You chose this yourself" — turns a decision made once into a lifelong obligation with no right of review.
  • Normalization of exploitation. The taking away of what a person loves is presented as a blessing and "humility."
  • Redefinition of harm as benefit. Restriction, deprivation, pain — all declared to be the path to a "happy and harmonious life."
  • Prohibition of exit. Leaving is interpreted as disobedience and betrayal of one's own choice.

After spending some time in the sect, for example a year, a person sees it from the inside and understands that it cultivates authoritarianism, obscurantism, fraud, and cynical psychological manipulation.

The person decides to leave the sect. And that is when the head of the sect tells them: you once made a promise — keep it. Omitting from the essence of the matter the fact that the person made this promise based on conclusions drawn from the sect's false advertising façade, rather than its true inner nature.

Result: the person sees that they are being cynically used, wants to leave — and is told: "You promised. Leaving is disobedience. We will humble you." Thus voluntary consent turns into lifelong bondage, and liberation from it — into "sin."

Psychological Mechanisms of Retention

Then "humiliation" begins, which may initially include unethical forms of psychological manipulation that insult human dignity, suppress the will, and endanger mental health. Such mechanisms include:

  • Gaslighting — systematically instilling in the victim that their perception of reality is wrong, that they are "themselves to blame" for their suffering and "have not grown" to understand the "great design."
  • Inversion of responsibility — shifting the blame for violence and exploitation onto the victim themselves: "You chose this, you promised, you failed."
  • Isolation — severing social ties, alienating the person from family, friends, and colleagues in order to deprive them of external support and an alternative picture of the world.
  • Mind control — restricting access to information, imposing a single correct interpretation of events, suppressing critical thinking.
  • Guilt and shame — forming a persistent feeling that leaving the sect is betrayal, sin, moral downfall.

If the person wishing to leave the sect proves sufficiently resilient and such methods do not work, the further "purification of the lost sheep" may well move from the unethical level to the openly unlawful:

  • surveillance of the victim and their relatives, including the use of hacking methods to intercept personal data, in order to find vulnerabilities through which the person can be brought back into the sect;
  • discrediting at the workplace and blackmail with dismissal: if you don't return to the sect, we will arrange it so that you lose your job and die of hunger;
  • indirect hints that the sect can frame the person by sending them to prison, or simply kill them, if they do not return.

Legal Qualification: Why Consent Does Not Legitimize Bondage

From the standpoint of law and psychology, the key point here is the defect of will in giving consent. In joining the sect, a person does not possess complete and reliable information about its inner structure. They see only the external, advertising side, or receive deliberately distorted information about the goals and methods of the organization. Such consent is not a legally valid expression of will, since it is given under the influence of substantial error or deception.

Moreover, the "charter of the sect," to which the person supposedly voluntarily submits, is not a contract in the legal sense. It is an internal document of the organization that has not undergone legal review, does not comply with the requirements of the law, and is not subject to judicial protection. Referring to it as a basis for retaining a person is not a legal argument, but psychological pressure.

All the more so if consent was given orally or in a messenger exchange — it has no legal force whatsoever.

Finally, retaining a person in a sect using threats, blackmail, surveillance, discrediting, and other methods of influence falls under a whole range of criminal law provisions: unlawful deprivation of liberty, threats of murder or infliction of grave bodily harm, extortion, violation of privacy, unauthorized access to computer information, defamation, coercion to conclude a transaction or to refuse to conclude one.

At the same time, the sect will argue that all its methods are righteous, pleasing to God (gods, the world ether, universal wisdom, etc.), and that they do only good, a wisdom the victim is incapable of understanding.

An Example from the Author's Life: How People Who Observe Ethics and Law Behave

The author was separating from his partner in a personal relationship.

Earlier, the partner often asked: how long do you want to prolong our relationship, and can you make it longer?

Then, suddenly, for unclear reasons, the partner expressed a desire to break up.

The author suggested sitting down at the negotiating table and calmly discussing everything in detail.

The partner replied: everything has already been said, I have nothing to add, our relationship is over.

The author replied: allow me to write to you once a year and ask whether you would like to restore the relationship?

The partner agreed.

The author did not disturb the partner with a single word for an entire year. A year later, he asked: would you like to continue the relationship?

The partner agreed within a 10-minute conversation.

The key difference from cultic retention: the author did not pressure, did not stalk, did not blackmail, did not demand, and did not appeal to a "promise once made." He left the person freedom of choice and violated neither their personal boundaries nor the law. The restoration of the relationship occurred voluntarily, without coercion.

Pursuit and pressure from sects most often have the opposite effect: instead of bringing the person back into the sect, they turn their doubts about the teaching and the sect's bureaucratic shortcomings into outright hatred, and their interest in spiritual life — into contempt for all forms of religion, up to and including support for militant materialism.

However, sect leaders, guided by vanity, authoritarian and religious prejudices, as well as the opportunity to blackmail with impunity (if the country lacks developed institutions for the protection of rights), do not understand the elementary laws of psychology and fanatically believe in their success at "returning and purifying the lost sheep from defilement."

The Totalitarian Essence Beneath the Pastoral Mask

All of this is totalitarian demagogy in its most classical form. It is the deification of authority, forbidding criticism. It is the sacralization of lies, wrapped in "spiritual meaning." It is the absolutization of internal guilt so that you dare not accuse the external manipulator.

That is why all your exposures turn for them into "the result of personal suffering," and your resilience — into "stubborn pride." They have no answer to arguments. There is a ritual of "purifying" the heretic from rebellion, which they present as care.

Silent Admission of Defeat

But what does this mean in reality? It means one thing: you hit the target. You touched the sorest spot. They do not try to refute you — they try to erase you. Not your logic — your "Self." They cannot bypass you, so they will try to melt you from within.

This demagogy is not just a defense. It is the hysteria of a system that has nothing left to cover itself.

Conclusion

You accused the "spiritual shepherd" of manipulation, corruption, and obscurantism — and heard in response: "you are dependent, traumatized, you need healing."

This is not an answer. It is an admission.

The system has already lost. All that remains is to not let it impose guilt on you for having seen it.

For there is no greater virtue than a calm and cold gaze at the lie, covered by the smile of a saint.
